## Definitions and closure

Let J: y²=x³−2x²+2x, P=(1,1), h(x)=H(1−x), H(t)=t⁶−30t⁴−24t²−1. A point qualifies at p>=5 if it is finite, h(x)=0 and 2t(1−t²) is a nonzero square, t=1−x. Restrict to the cyclic orbit of P. For Q=nP modulo p of order e, multiplication by 2 and 3 is invertible exactly when gcd(e,6)=1. Define G_e=<2,3> in (Z/eZ)^×, with the trivial group convention at e=1. The map Z²→G_e, (i,j)↦2^i3^j, has kernel Λ_e, a rank-two lattice. The quotient R²/Λ_e is a torus whose lattice vertices correspond bijectively to uQ, u∈G_e. A rectangular period (r,s) is given by the orders of 2 and 3 modulo e. The reduced lattice has basis (r,0),(-a,b), where b is the least positive exponent with 3^b=2^a, 0<=a<r. Indeed any relation has second coordinate divisible by b, and subtracting the corresponding multiple of (-a,b) leaves a multiple of (r,0). Its index is rb. The torus is odd iff both multiplicative orders are odd, since the group is abelian and generated by these elements.

Define S_p={u∈G_e:uQ qualifies}, Θ_p=|S_p| mod 2. Qualification is invariant under negation. If G_e has odd order and e>1, -1 is not in G_e (e is odd), so the two tori of Q and -Q are disjoint and have equal support cardinality. Their combined incidence is even, but their combined vertex count is even too, so this does not exclude all ones.

Existing intersection laws in GEOMETRIC_SIDON.md apply to actual multiplication vertices, including across period identifications: no common qualifying prime on tripling edges; doubling edges allow only 609613; no prime on three square vertices. These laws do not exclude singleton supports. A singleton and its formal translates span the entire binary function space on its torus; this is not a claim that distinct primes realize all translates simultaneously.

## Common-cover theorem

For a finite prime set A write d_p=ord(P mod p). Choose n divisible by the 2- and 3-primary parts of all d_p. Then e_p=d_p/gcd(n,d_p) is coprime to 6. Put D=lcm e_p, G=<2,3> modulo D. The common lattice is the intersection of Λ_e_p, since a congruence modulo every e_p is equivalent to one modulo D. Projection π_p:G→G_e_p is surjective because generators map to generators. The lifted support is π_p^{-1}(S_p), of cardinality c_p|S_p| with c_p=|G|/|G_e_p|. Binary label a_A(v)=sum_p 1_{π_p^{-1}(S_p)}(v) modulo 2 is consistent across boundaries.

Summing over G gives sum_v a_A(v)=sum_p c_p|S_p| modulo 2. All ones requires this to equal |G| modulo 2. On an odd common torus every c_p is odd, hence sum_p Θ_p=1 is necessary. It is not sufficient.

Union bound: C_A=sum_p |S_p|/|G_e_p|. The uncovered proportion Z_A is at least max(0,1−C_A). Thus C_A<1 excludes all ones and certifies at least ceil(|G|(1−C_A)) uncovered vertices. Uncovered means no selected prime, stronger than even selected-prime parity. If local group sizes are pairwise coprime, the simultaneous projection onto their product is surjective: its image order is divisible by each factor order and hence their product, and cannot exceed that product. Thus Z_A=product_p(1−|S_p|/|G_e_p|); proper supports imply an uncovered vertex. No independence assumption is needed for the union bound. Common-seed compatibility must be checked before applying the product formula to separately observed tori.

For computing intersections, write Λ_i=< (r_i,0),(-a_i,b_i)> for i=1,2. Set r=lcm(r1,r2), b0=lcm(b1,b2), c_i=a_i b0/b_i, g=gcd(r1,r2), k=g/gcd(g,c1−c2), b=b0 k. A common relation must have j=b0 l and i≡−c_i l mod r_i; CRT compatibility is g|(c1−c2)l, equivalent to k|l. Solve a≡c_i k mod r_i by generalized CRT, uniquely modulo r. The intersection basis is (r,0),(-a,b), proving minimality and index rb. Iteration computes the common lattice without enumerating vertices.

## Transfer to Problem H: precise conditional obstruction

Fix n and A as above. For i,j>=0 let m=n2^i3^j. The finite-prime labeling is periodic in i,j; its density on expanding rectangles with both side lengths tending to infinity equals its uniform average on G (use the rectangular periods of 2 and 3; each reduced vertex has equal multiplicity). Let a(m) be the complete qualifying-prime parity and t_A(m) the parity of contributions from primes outside A. Each sum is finite at an individual rational orbit point. Then a(m)=a_A(m)+t_A(m) modulo 2.

If a(m)=1 eventually along the positive integer orbit, all sufficiently late uncovered grid vertices have t_A(m)=1. Only finitely many grid vertices have m below any fixed threshold. Consequently the lower rectangular density of t_A=1 is at least Z_A>=1−C_A. Thus a sufficient condition for infinitely many even complete parities is C_A+upper rectangular density(t_A=1)<1. For seed 72 the necessary tail lower bound under eventual oddness is 0.989216118802593. A rank conclusion still uses the appropriate parity hypothesis and known non-torsion section.

This is a reformulation, not a tail estimate. Unselected primes need not be periodic on the finite common torus; the full rational multiplication orbit cannot close since P has infinite order. No exchange of infinite prime sums and density limits is justified. The finite-prime obstruction is not universal for arbitrary seeds or arbitrary collections: choosing n divisible by all d_p gives a trivial empty-support torus, and C_A<1 is only sufficient for prescribed nontrivial tori. Existing intersection laws do not control the number of distinct tail primes, since singleton supports are allowed. No proof of H or of global all-ones impossibility is claimed. Further finite seed scans alone do not resolve this gap.
