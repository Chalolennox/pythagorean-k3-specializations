## Verdict
The conjectured reflection formula (6) is true for every rational t = a/b outside {0, ±1, ∞} (with (0/1) = 1), shared bad primes included, and quadratic reciprocity is not needed. It is a corollary of F.28 and Appendix B, equivalent to F.25(b) with the Jacobi numerator written as -J(a,b) = d²·a_P b_P (d = gcd(b²-a², 2ab, a²+b²)). It gives no leverage on Problem H: it is a product of two signs that still contains the parity factor (-1)^(ω(R_t)+ω(R_u)).

## Proof (set t = a/b, u = (t+1)/(t-1) = a_u/b_u in lowest terms, J = 2ab(a²-b²))
1. Let p ≥ 5 divide H(a,b). Then p ∤ c₄ (F.28) and p | Δ, so c₄³ ≡ c₆² (mod p) and c₄ = (c₆/c₄)² is a nonzero square mod p. Modulo squares c₄ ≡ -3(b²-a²)(a²+b²) and -c₆ ≡ -6ab(a²+b²) (13824 = 6·48²), so (-c₆/p) = (2ab/p)(-3(a²+b²)/p) = (2ab(b²-a²)/p) = (-J/p), and w_p(E_t) = -(-J/p). This is the note's C(a,b) = -6ab(a²+b²).
2. For u: J(a+b, a-b) = 4J(a,b), so J(a_u,b_u) is in the class of J(a,b), and w_p(E_u) = -(-J/p) for p | H(a_u,b_u). A prime in both R_t and R_u contributes (-1)²(-J/p)² = 1, so no coprimality hypothesis is needed. Product over the multiplicative primes: (-1)^(ω(R_t)+ω(R_u))·(-J/(R_tR_u)).
3. For p ≥ 5 dividing a²+b²: a_u²+b_u² = 2(a²+b²)/g², so v_p agrees; by Appendix B (Rizzo's Table I) the types are good, III*, I₀*, III for v_p ≡ 0,1,2,3 (mod 4) and w_p = (2/p)^v for both curves; the product is 1.
4. Real places give +1; every other prime ≥ 5 has good reduction; 2 and 3 give l(t).
Explicit l(t) = c₂c₃ (A = (b²-a²)/(a²+b²), B = 2ab/(a²+b²) mod 16 and mod 9): c₂ = -1 iff (A,B) mod 16 is in {(0,15),(1,0),(4,9),(7,4),(7,12),(9,4),(12,7),(12,9)}; c₃ = -1 iff (A,B) mod 9 is in {(1,6),(3,8)}.

## The cross-character
(2) C_tC_u ≡ J (mod squares) is a one-line computation (class of -6ab·D times -3(a²-b²)·2D). (5) χ_cross = 1: from (2) and step 1, (C_t/p) = (-1/p) for p | R_u and (C_u/p) = (-1/p) for p | R_t, so (C_t/R_u)(C_u/R_t) = (-1/R_tR_u). At a prime dividing both R_t and R_u this forces (J/p) = 1 (for example t = -3, p = 7, J = -48 ≡ 1).
