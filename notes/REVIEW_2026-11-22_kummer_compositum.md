# The qualifying-root field L*, the parity characters a_j + i, and where they live

Status: derived with a language model, 22 November 2026; no human has checked it. No consequence for Problem H; the paper is not changed. Code: code/verify_kummer_compositum.py (standard library only; about 6 seconds, `--quick` under 1 second). Data: data/kummer_rank_certificate.txt. This note builds on notes/REVIEW_2026-11-22_support_structure_proved.md (pull request 81), whose Propositions 6 to 8 it refers to.

Labels as in that note: "Proof given" is a proof written out here, derived with a language model and not read by a human, with the finite part checked by code; "Observed" is a finite computation. The Magma runs described in the two reviewer dossiers (not in the repo) were not rerun; every number below was reproduced in Python or by exact rational arithmetic.

## Origin
Two independent review dossiers of 22 November 2026 (Magma V2.30-1 logs supplied by the owner) computed: the resolvents R12 and R24, [N:M] = 64 for N = M(sqrt(a_j + i), sqrt(a_j - i)), and then [NF:F] = 8 for the "old" field F defined by the radicals q, r, x below. A final reviewer comment concluded that the degree-eight layer does not distinguish qualifying roots on the completely split locus, and proposed to close the branch. This note records what holds, one correction, and why that last conclusion needs a scope remark.

## Definitions
H(t) = t^6 - 30t^4 - 24t^2 - 1 = U(t^2), U(s) = s^3 - 30s^2 - 24s - 1; roots +-a_1, +-a_2, +-a_3 with a_1 a_2 a_3 = 1 and s_j = a_j^2. N = ℚ(a_j) is the splitting field of H (Gal(N/ℚ) = S_4, order 24, paper, Appendix E), M = N(i) (i is not in N), so [M:ℚ] = 48. Over M, with the radicands
q(a) = 2a(1 - a^2)/(1 + a^2)^2, r(a) = (1 - a)(1 + a^2), x(a) = 1 - a (all six roots a),
the old field is F = M(sqrt q(a), sqrt r(a), sqrt x(a)) and the new field is N' = M(sqrt(a + i), sqrt(a - i)).

## Proposition K1 (F is the field L* of F.5). Proof given
F = L*. Proof. Over M, [-1] is trivial, and [q(a)] = [2a] + [1 - a] + [1 + a], [r(a)] = [1 - a] + [1 + a^2]. The classes of F.5 over N are 1 - a_i, 1 + a_i, 1 + a_i^2, -1 and g(a_i) = 2a_i(1 - a_i^2) with [g] = [2][a_i][1 - a_i][1 + a_i]. Both lists span the same subgroup of M*/M*^2: from [1 - a], [1 + a], [1 + a^2] and [g] one recovers [2a], and conversely. Since ∏(1 + s_j) = -U(-1) = 8 and ∏ 2a_j = 8, the class [2] is in the span, so [a_j] is too. [] The degree agrees with F.5: [L*:N] = 2^10, i not in N, hence [F:M] = 2^9, [F:ℚ] = 48 * 512 = 24576 = |G|.

## Proposition K2 (the two relations and the rank 9). Proof given; lower bound by a finite-field certificate
Reduced generating list of the old classes (11 elements): 2, a_1, a_2, 1 - a_j and 1 + a_j (j = 1, 2, 3), 1 + a_1^2, 1 + a_2^2 (the others follow from [a_3] = [a_1] + [a_2] and [1 + s_1] + [1 + s_2] + [1 + s_3] = [2]).

(a) Exact identity: s(a) = a(26 + 29a^2 - a^4)/3 satisfies s(a)^2 = 3(a^4 - 1) modulo H(a) (checked in the code with rational arithmetic; it is the identity behind (R1), (R2) of F.57 (b') and code/verify_group_G.py). So -3(1 - a^4) is a square in ℚ(a) already, with the explicit root s(a).
(b) The masks 993 and 1433 of the finite-field kernel are the products 2(1 - s_2)(1 - s_3)(1 + s_1) and 2(1 - s_1)(1 - s_3)(1 + s_2). Since ∏(1 - s_j) = U(1) = -54, the first equals -108(1 + s_1)/(1 - s_1) = (6/(1 - s_1))^2 * (-3)(1 - s_1^2), a square times -3(1 - a_1^4), hence a square by (a); the second is the same with a_2. So dim V_old <= 11 - 2 = 9.
(c) Lower bound: for each prime p = 1 mod 4 that splits H completely there are 48 embeddings of M into F_p (6 orderings of the roots of U, 4 sign choices with a_1 a_2 a_3 = 1, 2 choices of i; all are genuine because Gal(N/ℚ) is the full group of order 24 and i is not in N). A square-class relation over M must hold under every embedding, so the F_2-rank of the matrix of Legendre bits is a lower bound. Over the 512 split primes below 3*10^5 (24576 rows) the rank is 9 and its kernel consists exactly of the masks 993, 1433 and 993 xor 1433 = 1656. A 12-row certificate from the primes 557, 1229, 1601 is in data/kummer_rank_certificate.txt (12 rows, columns 0..13, rank 12 on all columns and 9 on the first eleven).
Hence dim V_old = 9 and [F:M] = 2^9. []

Correction: one of the dossiers states prod(1 - s_j) = +54 in a review comment. The value is U(1) = -54; the sign does not affect any square class over M (i is in M), but the repository formulas use -54.

## Proposition K3 (the degree-eight layer). Proof given
[N'F : F] = 8. With a_j + i and a_j - i: (a_j + i)(a_j - i) = 1 + a_j^2 is in V_old, and [-a_j + i] = [a_j - i], so N'F is F with sqrt(a_j + i), j = 1, 2, 3, adjoined and the relative rank is at most 3. The same finite-field matrix with the three extra columns has rank 12, so the rank is exactly 12 and [N'F : F] = 8. Degrees: [N'F : M] = 2^12 = 4096, [N'F : ℚ] = 48 * 4096 = 196608, [N' : M] = 2^6 and [N' ∩ F : M] = 2^3. Gal(N'F/F) is (Z/2)^3, and Gal(F/ℚ) acts on it through S_4 -> S_3 by permuting the three coordinates (modulo V_old, [a - i] = [a + i] + [1 + a^2] = [a + i]). Over primes splitting completely in F the Frobenius conjugacy classes are the weight classes 0, 1, 2, 3 of F_2^3, of relative sizes 1/8, 3/8, 3/8, 1/8. The absolute density of an individual labelled sign pattern is not 1/196608; only complete splitting in N'F has that density.

## Remark K4 (where these fields live: p = 1 mod 8). Proof given; checked
(a) [2] is in V_old: a prime that splits completely in F has chi(2) = 1, i.e. p = 1 mod 8. The same holds for the smaller field M(sqrt q, sqrt r) generated by the criterion without x(a): its nine classes q_j, r_j^+, r_j^- (= [2a_j(1 - a_j^2)], [(1 - a_j)(1 + a_j^2)], [(1 + a_j)(1 + a_j^2)]) are independent (rank 9, checked: the maximum possible), adding [2] leaves the rank at 9 (since ∏ q_j has class [-432] = [3] and ∏ r_j^+ r_j^- has class [-54] = [6]), and adding the three [a_j + i] gives 12. So a prime with p = 5 mod 8 never has all six roots qualifying; this is the same fact as the congruence p = 1 mod 24 for |S| = 12 in Proposition 4 of the support-structure note.
(b) The parity law (Proposition 6 (b), (c) there) holds at p = 5 mod 8. At p = 1 mod 8 the parity of the index is not read off, but P is in 2C(F_p) and Proposition 6 (e) applies: the character chi(a + i) is constant on the orbit, so a root a is accessible only if chi(a + i) = +1 on the nP branch and chi(a + i) = chi(-(1 + i)) on the nP + T branch. See K5.
(c) The relevant Galois statement for p = 5 mod 8 is at a single root. Over the degree-one primes of ℚ(a, i) (a a root of H, i^2 = -1, in F_p), the five characters of g(a), 1 - a, 1 + a, 1 + a^2 and 2 are independent and chi(a + i) is none of their 32 products; this was verified with explicit witnesses over 2256 such primes below 20000 (it is a complete argument for these five classes: a relation would hold at every degree-one prime). So the parity of the index at a root a, which equals chi(a + i) by Proposition 6, is not forced by the qualifying characters.
(d) Over the 512 primes below 3*10^5 that split completely in M, the weight of the six bits chi(a_j +- i) is odd exactly when p = 5 mod 8 (231 primes = 1 mod 8 with even weight, 281 primes = 5 mod 8 with odd weight; checked in the code), because the product of the twelve radicands a_j +- i is 8. Further orbit statistics of Gal(M/Q) on the six bits (ten orbits, frequencies consistent with uniform) were computed in a scratch script that is not in the repository and are not claimed here.

## Proposition K5 (the parity characters restrict accessibility at p = 1 mod 8). Proof given; checked on the 106 search cases
Let p be a prime splitting completely in F (so p = 1 mod 8 and all six roots a satisfy the qualifying characters; then chi(1 + a^2) = +1 on branch 0 and chi(-a + i) = chi(a - i) = chi(a + i)). Let w_0 be the number of j in {1, 2, 3} with chi(a_j + i) = -1, and w_1 the number with chi(a_j + i) = -chi(-(1 + i)). By Proposition 6 (e) a pair +-a_j can contribute indices to S_{p,0} (resp. S_{p,1}) only if it is not counted by w_0 (resp. w_1). Each pair contributes at most four indices, so |S_{p,j}| <= 4(3 - w_j). The weight w_j is the S_3-invariant of the three parity bits, so the weight classes of K3 (proportions 1/8, 3/8, 3/8, 1/8) are exactly the classes that bound the support: w = 0 allows up to 12, w = 1 up to 8, w = 2 up to 4, w = 3 gives S empty. In particular |S_{p,j}| = 12 needs w_j = 0, one of the eight sign patterns. Checked: for the 53 primes of the search to 3*10^7 (106 prime-branch cases) there is no violation of the bound and 45 equalities; the only case with |S| = 12 (p = 5251537, branch 1) has w_1 = 0. The new character layer therefore does carry accessibility information, and this corrects the statement of an earlier version of this note that the characters do not constrain membership.

This is a necessary condition only: it does not make membership in <P> a Chebotarev condition, since the remaining condition is membership of Q in <P> inside 2C(F_p), a subgroup-membership question.

## What this does and does not give
It confirms F.5's degree independently, identifies the two relations with F.57 (b'), and shows that the parity characters add three independent square classes over the qualifying field. At p = 1 mod 8 they bound the support (K5); at p = 5 mod 8 they give the parity of the index (Proposition 6 (b), (c) of the support-structure note), and at a single root they are independent of the qualifying characters (K4 (c)). It gives no infinite family of primes with an incompatibility (Open 1 of the support-structure note) and is no step toward Problem H. What remains is accessibility beyond this necessary condition: whether the point over a root lies in <P mod p>.
