# Angular Haar-measure symmetry for an elliptic-curve orbit

## Statement for independent review

Let
\[
C/\mathbb Q:\quad y^2=x^3-2x^2+2x=x\bigl((x-1)^2+1\bigr),
\]
with identity \(O\), rational two-torsion point \(T=(0,0)\), and \(P=(1,1)\). For a real point \(Q\in C(\mathbb R)\), except at the points where the coordinate is undefined, set
\[
t(Q)=1-x(Q),\qquad
z(Q)=\frac{1+i\,t(Q)}{1-i\,t(Q)}=e^{i\theta(Q)}.
\]
At \(O\), define \(z(O)=-1\) by continuity (corresponding to \(t=\infty\)). Let \(m\) denote normalized Haar probability measure on the compact real Lie group \(C(\mathbb R)\), and let \(\nu=z_*m\) be its pushforward to the unit circle. For each integer \(k\), define
\[
\mu_k=\int_{C(\mathbb R)}z(Q)^k\,dm(Q)
      =\int_{|z|=1}z^k\,d\nu(z).
\]

**Theorem.** The measure \(\nu\) is invariant under the reflection
\[
\mathcal R(z)=-i\,\overline z,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\theta\longmapsto-\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
Consequently, for every integer \(k\),
\[
\boxed{\mu_k=(-i)^k\overline{\mu_k}.}
\]
In particular, \(\mu_{4j}\) is real, \(\mu_{4j+2}\) is purely imaginary, \(\operatorname{Im}\mu_{4j+1}=-\operatorname{Re}\mu_{4j+1}\), and \(\operatorname{Im}\mu_{4j+3}=\operatorname{Re}\mu_{4j+3}\).

### Proof

**1. Translation by the two-torsion point.** For a point \(Q=(x,y)\) with \(x\ne0\), the line through \(Q\) and \(T=(0,0)\) has slope \(y/x\). The addition law on \(C\), in Weierstrass form with \(a_2=-2\), gives
\[
x(Q+T)=\left(\frac yx\right)^2+2-x
=\frac{x^3-2x^2+2x}{x^2}+2-x
=\frac2x.
\]
The identity extends to the remaining points in the natural projective sense, exchanging \(T\) and \(O\). In particular, translation by \(T\) is an involution.

**2. Induced transformation of the rational circle parameter.** Since \(x=1-t\),
\[
t(Q+T)=1-\frac{2}{1-t(Q)}
=-\frac{1+t(Q)}{1-t(Q)}=:\tau(t(Q)).
\]
For real \(t\), put \(z(t)=(1+it)/(1-it)\). Direct algebra yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
z(\tau(t))
&=\frac{(1-t)-i(1+t)}{(1-t)+i(1+t)}\\
&=-i\,\frac{1-it}{1+it}
=-i\,\overline{z(t)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both sides extend continuously to the exceptional parameters on the projective real line. Thus
\[
\boxed{z(Q+T)=-i\,\overline{z(Q)}}
\]
for all \(Q\in C(\mathbb R)\).

**3. Haar invariance.** Normalized Haar measure on \(C(\mathbb R)\) is invariant under every translation, in particular \(Q\mapsto Q+T\). Hence for every continuous function \(f\) on the unit circle,
\[
\int f(z)\,d\nu(z)
=\int_{C(\mathbb R)}f(z(Q+T))\,dm(Q)
=\int f(-i\overline z)\,d\nu(z).
\]
This is precisely \(\mathcal R_*\nu=\nu\).

**4. Fourier identity.** Taking \(f(z)=z^k\),
\[
\mu_k=\int(-i\overline z)^k\,d\nu(z)
=(-i)^k\overline{\int z^k\,d\nu(z)}
=(-i)^k\overline{\mu_k}.
\]
The listed phase restrictions follow by considering \(k\) modulo four. \(\square\)

## Connection to the orbit \(nP\)

The theorem above concerns Haar measure and does **not** require a hypothesis about \(P\). To identify \(\mu_k\) with the limit of the unsigned orbit averages
\[
\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N z(nP)^k\longrightarrow\mu_k,
\]
one additionally needs the cyclic subgroup generated by \(P\) to be dense in \(C(\mathbb R)\). The real curve has exactly one connected component because \(x((x-1)^2+1)\) has just one real root. Thus \(C(\mathbb R)\) is a circle group, and **if \(P\) is of infinite order**, its orbit is dense and equidistributed by the irrational-rotation theorem. The point \(P\) is indeed of infinite order: on the integral model, the duplication formula gives \(2P=(1/4,-5/8)\); by the Lutz–Nagell theorem, a rational torsion point on an integral short/general Weierstrass model has integral coordinates, so \(P\) cannot be torsion (if it were, \(2P\) would be torsion). Therefore the orbit averages converge to the stated Haar coefficients.

**Important distinction.** The involution maps \(nP\) to \(nP+T\), which generally belongs to a different coset of the cyclic subgroup. Haar invariance yields a symmetry of the *limiting unsigned measure*, not a pointwise symmetry of the finite orbit, nor any identity for global root numbers \(W(E_{t(nP)})\).

## Questions for the independent reviewer

1. Verify the addition-law formula \(x(Q+T)=2/x(Q)\) and the projective exceptional cases.
2. Verify the identity \(z(\tau(t))=-i\overline{z(t)}\), including its extension at \(t=1\) and \(t=\infty\).
3. Verify that the Haar measure on \(C(\mathbb R)\), rather than uniform measure in \(\theta\), is the appropriate limit for the orbit \(nP\).
4. Check the use of Lutz–Nagell on the integral Weierstrass equation and the deduction that \(P\) has infinite order.
5. Distinguish the proved unsigned symmetry from any unproved signed/root-number analogue.
